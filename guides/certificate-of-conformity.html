What is a Certificate of Conformity (CoC)?

Updated 18 November 2026

A Certificate of Conformity, often called a CoC, is issued by the local authority (or the Urban Development Authority in its areas) when a building has been completed according to its approved plan.

Why it matters

The CoC shows the building matches its approved plan. It is commonly needed to register an apartment’s condominium deed, and banks and buyers often ask for it.

Buying a finished apartment

Ask the seller or developer for a copy of the CoC and check the date and address match the building.

Buying off-plan

A building under construction will not have a CoC yet. Ask the developer when they expect it and what your agreement says if it is delayed.

Houses and annexes

Extensions and annexes also need approval. If an annex was added later, ask whether it was approved and covered by a CoC.

If there is no CoC

Buying without one carries risk: you may struggle to get a loan, register the deed or resell. Ask your lawyer to check with the local authority before you pay.

Common questions

Who issues a Certificate of Conformity?

The local authority, such as the municipal or urban council, or the Urban Development Authority in areas it covers.

Can I buy an apartment without a CoC?

You can, but it is riskier. You may have trouble with the deed, a loan or resale, so get legal advice first.

Does an annex need a CoC?

If the annex was built or extended with approval, it should have one. Unapproved structures can cause problems when selling or borrowing.
