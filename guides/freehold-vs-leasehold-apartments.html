Freehold vs leasehold apartments in Sri Lanka

Updated 18 November 2026

Two apartments in the same area can be priced very differently because of how they are owned. Before you compare prices, find out whether each one is freehold or leasehold.

Freehold

With a freehold apartment you own your unit with no end date, together with a share of the building’s common areas. You can sell it or leave it to your family.

Leasehold

With a leasehold apartment you have the right to use the unit for a fixed number of years. When the lease ends, the rights return to the landowner unless it is extended. The fewer years left, the lower the value usually is.

How it affects you

Freehold units usually cost more but are easier to finance and resell. Banks may lend less, or for a shorter term, on leasehold units with few years left.

Condominium registration

For a freehold apartment, ask whether the building has a registered condominium plan, so your unit can have its own deed.

Service charges

Most apartment buildings have a management corporation that collects monthly service charges for security, lifts, cleaning and repairs. Ask how much they are and whether there are arrears on the unit.

Common questions

Is freehold better than leasehold?

Freehold gives you permanent ownership and is usually easier to resell and finance. Leasehold can be cheaper and suit buyers who plan to stay for a set time.

How do I know if an apartment is freehold?

Ask the seller for the deed and have your lawyer confirm the type of title and, for a condominium, that the condominium plan is registered.

What happens when a lease ends?

The rights return to the landowner unless the lease is extended, so check how many years remain before you buy.
