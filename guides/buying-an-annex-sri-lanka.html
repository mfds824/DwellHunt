Buying an annex in Sri Lanka: deed, land share and approvals

Updated 18 November 2026

An annex usually sits on the same land as a main house, so buying one is different from buying a flat or a separate house. The key question is what exactly you will own.

What will you actually own?

Ask whether the annex comes with its own separate land and deed, or whether you would buy a share of land that is still part of the main property. An annex sold together with the main house is simpler. An annex on its own is only straightforward if the land has been properly divided and has its own title.

Deed and title

Ask your lawyer to check the deed and run a title search at the Land Registry, so you know the seller has clear title and there are no mortgages or disputes on the land.

Survey plan and boundaries

There should be a survey plan showing the land and the annex’s position. Walk the boundaries and compare them with the plan.

Building approvals

Ask for the approved building plan for the annex and, if it was built or extended, the Certificate of Conformity from the local authority. Unapproved annexes can cause problems with loans and resale.

Access

Make sure you have a legal right of way from the road to the annex, especially if you would pass through the main house’s land.

Utilities

Check whether electricity and water are on separate meters in the annex’s own name, or would need new connections.

Local-authority certificates

Your lawyer may also ask the local authority for street line, building line and non-vesting certificates to confirm the land is not affected by road widening or acquisition.

Common questions

Can I buy just an annex without the main house?

Only if the annex has, or will get, its own legally divided land and deed. Otherwise you would be buying a share of the whole property, which needs careful legal advice.

Can I get a home loan for an annex?

Banks usually want clear title, an approved plan and a valuation. An annex without its own deed may be hard to finance.

What should my lawyer check?

Title and title search, the deed, the survey plan, approvals and Certificate of Conformity, access rights and any local-authority certificates.
