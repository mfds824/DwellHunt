Rent advance and security deposit in Sri Lanka, explained

Updated 18 November 2026

Most long-term rentals in Sri Lanka ask for money before you move in: an advance and often a security deposit. They are different things, and mixing them up is the most common cause of disputes when a tenancy ends.

The advance

An advance is rent paid ahead of time, counted in months. It is normally used up against your rent: for example, a six-month advance on a one-year lease may cover the last six months. The number of months is usually negotiable, so ask.

The security deposit

A deposit is held to cover damage beyond normal wear, unpaid bills or missing items. It should be returned when you leave, minus any agreed deductions.

What to agree in writing

Write down the monthly rent, how many months of advance you pay and exactly which months it covers, the deposit amount, what can be deducted from it, and how many days after you leave it will be returned.

Protect yourself at move-in

Take dated photos of every room, list the furniture and appliances, and note existing damage. Ask the owner to sign the list. Keep receipts for every payment.

Getting the deposit back

Give notice as the agreement says, pay the final bills, return the keys and walk through the property with the owner against your move-in photos and list.

Common questions

How many months of advance is normal in Colombo?

It varies a lot by property and owner, from a few months to a year. Treat it as negotiable, especially for longer leases.

Is the advance refundable?

An advance is normally used against your rent rather than refunded. If you leave early, what happens to unused advance should be stated in the agreement.

Is the security deposit refundable?

Yes. It should be returned when you move out, less any deductions you agreed to in writing.
